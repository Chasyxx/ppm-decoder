Nielsen PPM CBET decoder 1.5

Nielsen PPM is a "hidden" identifier that can be embedded in a sound signal. One modulation method, CBET (CBET on SIGID wiki) (which could stand for "Critical Band Encoded Telemetry"?) goes from 1-3 kHz using 10 MFSK subcarriers and encoding 18 different 400ms long symbols across all of them. It'is used on FM and MW AM radio, and it tries to be strong enough to decode, while quiet enough to not affect casual listening. 2 symbols ([ and ]) are used for sync while the other 16 act as hexadecimal digits for a station ID and time code.

This is a PPM CBET decoder that seems to work. It uses an FFT to decode the symbols, syncronises the signal, and gives you the decoded ID and time code.

Press the button to start decoding. You could open your actual mic and put your radio next to it, but if you can use line in, or a virtual cable (SDR purposes), use that instead since you'll get a cleaner decode.
